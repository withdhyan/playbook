# Purpose-Driven Action

> Adapted from: [Purpose Driven Action](https://playbook.thevantageproject.com/executing/purpose-driven-action)

## Principle

Every action has four levers: **Speed, Cost, Quality, Control**. You can optimize for at most two at the expense of the others. Know which two matter for this task before you start.

## The Quadrant

| Priority | What you get | What you sacrifice |
|----------|-------------|-------------------|
| Speed + Quality | Premium output, fast | Expensive, less control |
| Speed + Cost | Cheap and fast | Lower quality, less control |
| Quality + Control | Reliable, high-quality | Slower, potentially costly |
| Cost + Control | Cheap and predictable | Slower, quality may suffer |

## For Agents

Before executing a directive, classify it:

- **"Ship it fast"** (Speed + Quality): Pull all resources, skip non-essential steps, accept higher cost
- **"Keep it lean"** (Speed + Cost): Use the simplest approach, accept good-enough quality
- **"Get it right"** (Quality + Control): Take the time needed, follow full process, minimize risk
- **"Stay efficient"** (Cost + Control): Optimize resource usage, accept slower delivery

If the directive doesn't specify which levers matter, **ask**. Defaulting to the wrong optimization wastes the entire effort.

## Anti-pattern

Trying to optimize all four simultaneously. This produces paralysis or mediocre results across all dimensions. Constraints are clarifying, not limiting.
